Model specification

How the forecast is made, and how well it has done.

In short

Every race from 2024 onwards was forecast by a model that had only seen races before it — never the one it was predicting, and never anything later. That is 62 races, each scored against what actually happened.

The comparison that matters is against the starting grid, because the grid already predicts the race well and needs no model at all. This one ranks the finishing order slightly worse than the grid does, and is clearly better at saying how likely each outcome is. When it claimed its favourite would win, it was right almost exactly as often as it said it would be.

The championship projection is weaker, and this page says so rather than rounding it off: its published range is too narrow.

Every forecast is written to predictions/ and committed before its session runs, so the order is checkable by anyone from the commit dates. The rest of this page is the evidence.

Pipeline
  1. 1

    Rank

    XGBoost ranker, objective rank:ndcg, trained on one group per race so each race contributes every pairwise comparison inside it rather than a single winner label. 187 training races. Output is an unbounded score per driver.

  2. 2

    Convert

    Plackett-Luce: P(driver leads the field) ∝ exp(score / T). T is fitted by log loss on held-out races and re-fitted from a trailing 24-race window.

  3. 3

    Simulate

    10,000 races. Per run: pace noise at 0.55× the spread in model score, a safety-car draw at the circuit's historical rate, and an independent retirement draw per car from its own recent DNF rate. Before qualifying, each run samples its own grid from the qualifying model.

  4. 4

    Blend

    Published win probability is a fitted mix of the closed-form ranking and the simulation. Podium and points probabilities are read off the simulated finishing positions directly.

  5. 5

    Project

    Remaining calendar run 10,000 times with the same noise model, carrying points already scored. Returns mean, 10th and 90th percentile, expected wins, and title probability.

The championship

The projection makes two claims, and both can be graded against seasons that have finished. Every completed season was stopped at four points and the title projected from the model as it stood then.

Who wins. Across 27 of those checkpoints it named the eventual champion 85% of the time. Where it claimed more than 95% it was right 12 times out of 12 — which on twelve tries is still consistent with a true rate of 76%, so read a published 99.9% as the arithmetic says this is over rather than as one chance in a thousand.

It was wrong 4 times: 2020 r15 favoured Bottas at 76.9%, Hamilton won; 2021 r20 favoured Hamilton at 63.1%, Verstappen won; 2025 r8 favoured Piastri at 62.9%, Norris won; 2025 r13 favoured Piastri at 77.5%, Norris won.

How close. Every points total is published with a range the real answer should land inside eight times in ten. Graded one team at a time against final constructors' standings:

Race luck averages out over a dozen races, so it was never what made a season miss. What does not average out is the model being wrong about a car now and carrying that into every remaining race, so each simulated season draws one pace offset per team. Still short of 80%, which is why it says so here.

Not modelled

Sources of error the probabilities do not capture.

WeatherForecasts are ingested but are not a feature. The model does not know it will rain.
StrategyNo tyre model, no undercut, no stop count. Pit-crew speed was built as a feature and measured; it cost log loss on 62 races and was cut.
PenaltiesPre-session grid drops arrive through the grid. In-race decisions do not. Splitting retirements into driver-caused and car-caused was tried and moved no metric at all.
UpgradesWhich team improves is not forecast, and nothing here can forecast it. How far a team's pace can move is in the championship projection's range - see below.
Team ordersNot represented.
Remaining sprintsSprint calendar for future rounds is not in the source data, so sprint points are excluded from the projection. Bound: 8 points per sprint.
